## Why GEMSDOE32 B2 may have ranked highest in the supplied list

**The causal explanation is not established.** The strongest direct evidence is mask geometry from local third-party mirror rasters, not an organizer receipt. The measured relationship recorded in the repo is: GEMSDOE25/GEMSDOE30 share the same parent positive support (44,090 dots); GEMSDOE32 B2 is a strict subset with 6,436 dots removed from the parent and none added; the removed locations are within 2 pixels of the mirrored known-fault raster. These are raster-measurement counts, not score results, and the mirror is not organizer-authenticated.

The plausible metric mechanism is marginal coverage versus false-positive cost. Under the official metric description (distance-weighted Tversky, α=0.2, β=0.8, triangular kernel radius 300 m), a prediction can add little recall if its nearby truth is already covered by the kernel, while still adding false-positive mass. Removing low-marginal-credit predictions can therefore improve the ratio **if** the recall lost is smaller than the false-positive penalty avoided. The reverse is also possible: if a removed dot covers a real held-out new fault, recall loss may dominate. A buffer around known faults is not automatically a free scoring exclusion; prior official-source notes say the masked area is pixel-exact.

The reported collar ladder is suggestive but uncontrolled: the value 0.2778 is USER-REPORTED / UNVERIFIED and no receipt here binds it to a hash. It does not prove that proximity-based thinning caused the improvement, that all removed pixels were false positives, or that another radius would be better. The detailed algebra and caveats are in [`knowledge/02_why_gemsdoe32_b2_scored_0.2778.md`](02_why_gemsdoe32_b2_scored_0.2778.md).

As a warning about calibration, the prior H-R1 control record reports **HOLDOUT-DTI 0.004729** (evaluator `gems57-pooled-hide-v2`, 45,260 withheld positives, 95% CI [0.004337, 0.005163]) for the GEMSDOE32 B2 raster. This is a catalogue-segment hide-and-recover result from a prior session, not an organizer score or a leaderboard estimate. Its large gap from the user-reported value shows why the present holdout cannot be used to forecast the private score.

**Can a new method exceed the reported 0.2778?** It is possible in principle; this repo has no verified experiment that establishes it. The defensible next step is not to infer a numerical projection from nine correlated historical entries, but to resolve the lane, authorized data and shared-template provenance, then validate a preregistered hypothesis with the exact shared evaluator and proper fold-local features.
