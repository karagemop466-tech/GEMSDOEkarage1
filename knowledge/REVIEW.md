# Scientific and engineering review

## What was actually verified
The initial checkout had only a 16-byte README. No pipeline, features, data, history of experiments, or lane was present. We retrieved nine public repository READMEs at commit-pinned refs via GitHub API and downloaded the nine historical TIFFs identified by the pinned GEMSDOE36 source. SHA-256 hashes and independent binary-mask comparisons are recorded in `docs/data/`. Source snapshots are third-party evidence, not scientific or organizer authority.

All nine historical masks exceed the requested directional three-pixel duplicate threshold against another of these nine. GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 have exactly equal positive-pixel masks despite different file bytes. H33-B2 has 37,654 dots; GEMSDOE36 has 38,854. An independent set comparison additionally confirms B2 is a subset of GEMSDOE25 with 6,436 dots removed, and GEMSDOE36 contains all B2 dots plus 1,200 (see `docs/data/b2_forensics.json`). These are **audit counts, not scores**. Continuous source surfaces are not available in these nine downloads, so no surface uniqueness certification was performed. This small historical registry can disqualify copies; it cannot certify uniqueness against all previous submissions.

## Why H33-B2 may have performed well
The requested historical ranking places B2 first, but no artifact-linked organizer receipt was supplied or independently retrieved. We therefore do not reproduce that number as a confirmed score or calibrate predictions from it. The pinned GEMSDOE33/36 narratives attribute the family to thinning and removal of catalogue-adjacent dots. Our comparisons independently establish high geometric similarity, not the causal effect of any ablation.

For a distance-tolerant metric, nearby predictions can provide redundant coverage. Removing redundant false-positive mass while retaining coverage can improve the denominator tradeoff. This is a plausible explanation, **not proof** that flank removal caused the historical result. The alternative is tuning to a particular hidden-label distribution. Ablations must keep features, spatial splits, dot budget, evaluation mask and metric version fixed. A buffer around visible faults for feature leakage control is NOT the same as broadening the scoring exclusion mask. Only the latter's specified pixel-exact mask should be used for scoring.

A fault score is not a geothermal-vent discovery metric. Magnetic or conductivity anomalies can indicate structure, lithology or alteration without active permeability, heat or fluid flow. Distinctive geological language does not prove predictive novelty. There is no defensible numerical leaderboard forecast here, nor evidence of beating either of the conflicting leaderboard targets in the request.

## Ranked hypotheses — unimplemented research slate, NOT lane assignments
All are untried **in this initially empty repository**; global novelty is NOT established. Ranking is qualitative expected utility per effort, not a numerical expected DTI gain. All require the designated cached stack and fold-safe testing; none is currently validated or execution-authorized.

| Priority | Hypothesis / layers | Physical transform and missing-fault rationale | Difference / confounder / cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cross-contact texture displacement; RTP/TMI and detrended elevation | Fit offset of texture patches across a candidate line after removing local planar trends; a coherent displacement can reveal a buried discontinuity without a visible scarp. | Tests displacement rather than gradient magnitude or thinning. Lithologic boundaries or erosional terraces can mimic offsets. Medium cost; raw cached layer semantics must first be confirmed. |
| 2 | Conductivity boundary curvature paired with basement depth; cond_surf and depth_to_base_surf | Detect changes in curvature of conductivity interfaces aligned with basement-depth flexure, suppressing broad basin-wide trends; potentially targets concealed basin-margin breaks. | Uses interface bending rather than high conductivity alone. Salinity boundaries and sediment compaction mimic it. Medium cost; may overlap existing depth/conductivity lanes, so registry review mandatory. |
| 3 | Magnetic edge phase reversal across terrain breaks; RTP/TMI and detrended elevation | Compare signed cross-line profiles at several scales; coincident phase reversal and topographic break could identify offsets even where continuous edge strength is weak. | Signed profile shape rather than edge amplitude; dikes and remanent magnetization are confounders. Medium/high cost; global novelty unproven. |
| 4 | Local radiometric mixing departure across a structure; K/Th/U plus RTP | Fit local background mixtures and find narrow residual bands aligned with structural anomalies; alteration could expose a fluid pathway absent from surface mapping. | Residual composition rather than a raw ratio. ## Shared-tool and data investigation
GEMSDOE32 exposes `scripts/evaluate_holdout.py` and `scripts/submission_writer.py`; the evaluator delegates to `src/gems32/holdout.py`. It accepts static masks as well as per-cell OOF predictions. A static candidate generated with all-catalogue features must not be treated as leakage-safe merely because the scorer hides labels. The feature receipt points to `.cache/gems_work/feature_stack.npy`, not a cached stack in this checkout. Its data manifest describes GitHub mirror parts for training features, labels and sample template; those pins are not organizer authentication. Thus **data is potentially recoverable through GitHub**, and claiming all data retrieval is impossible would be incorrect. Restore it only after identifying the designated template and lane; verify byte pins and band semantics, then reuse its tools unchanged.

The organizer's public reference README was fetched from GitHub. It explicitly supports CPU-only execution; a GPU is not an absolute training prerequisite. No DrivenData login or public-page access was tested here: those hosts are outside the environment's allowlist. We cannot confirm a current leaderboard, weekly cap, official downloadable data licensing, or portal acceptance.
