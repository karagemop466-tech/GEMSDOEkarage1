## 3. Why the removal can raise the score

The official metric is the distance-weighted Tversky index, with α = 0.2 and β = 0.8 and a triangular 3 px kernel. Local definition (`vendor/gems57/metric.py`):

DTI = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2·FP_w + 0.8·FN_w)

Two consequences follow from this form. These are algebra, not a fit:

1. **Removing a dot that earns no credit always raises DTI.** A pure false positive lowers FP_w by (1 − k) with k = 0. The denominator drops by 0.2, the numerator is unchanged, so DTI goes up.
2. **Adding a non-redundant dot with credit k helps only if k > α·DTI.** At DTI ≈ 0.28 the threshold is k > 0.056. Equivalently, removing a dot with credit k below this threshold helps.

The organizer's initial round scores faults "not contained within the current public USGS database" (ORGANIZER-PUBLIC, DrivenData problem page). If that is right, a dot 1–2 px from a mapped catalogue trace probably earns little or no credit. Removing it is then close to case 1. This is a **mechanism hypothesis**. Its magnitude cannot be computed locally, because the true TP and FN counts are not available. It fits the sign of the reported +0.0178 but does not prove it.

## 5. Can a unique submission above 0.2778 be built?

**Not demonstrated. We cannot claim it.** The reasons:

1. **No local measurement ranks candidates for the official round.** HOLDOUT-DTI ordering contradicts the public ordering (B2 0.0047 holdout against 0.2778 public; GEMSDOE25 0.1023 holdout against 0.2600 public). A better local score would not show a better leaderboard score.
2. **Uniqueness.** B2 is a strict subset of GEMSDOE25. Its forward overlap (its dots within 3 px of another file's dots) is 0.9988 to 1.0 against the other eight focus files (`forward_dot_overlap_matrix_3px`, row GEMSDOE32). Against the registry calibration lattice `r13-lattice-s5` it is 0.99915. The literal 70% gate can never be passed by a 37,654-dot set against that lattice, since the minimum possible overlap at that budget is 82.34%.
3. **Any pruning variant is a near-clone.** A stronger halo rule (for example 3 px instead of 2 px) changes only a few thousand dots. It would still overlap the parent at more than 0.85.
4. **The public-board numbers need checking.** The request cited 0.3195 and 0.4413–0.4544 as the top. The 30 Oct re-read (ORGANIZER-PUBLIC) gives #1 = 0.4544, 0.3195 at rank #48, and 0.4413 **absent from the top 50**. The 0.4413 figure is therefore unconfirmed (IR-L2 below).

**What would settle it:** an organizer-scored submission with a receipt, on a file that passes the gates. Neither is available here. This session has used 1 of 3 experiments (E1); no weekly slot has been selected or spent.
