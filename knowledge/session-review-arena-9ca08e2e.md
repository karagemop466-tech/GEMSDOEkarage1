### 3.2 First-Principles Calculus of Pooled Distance-Weighted Tversky ($\alpha = 0.2, \beta = 0.8, R = 300\text{ m}$)

Per the [official metric specification](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/#performance-metric) and [organizer forum clarification](https://community.drivendata.org/t/scoring-clarification-are-known-usgs-ingenious-faults-masked-when-scoring-and-are-they-in-the-final-round-label-set/11516), known USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue pixels $C$ are masked during evaluation, while unmapped fault pixels $G \subset F \setminus C$ are scored using a $300\text{ m}$ ($3\text{ px}$ at $100\text{ m/px}$) linear distance-decay kernel $K(d) = \max(0, 1 - d/300)$. Writing pooled weighted true-positive, false-positive, and false-negative masses over scored pixels $S = F \setminus C$ as $\text{TP}_w, \text{FP}_w, \text{FN}_w$ (with $\text{TP}_w + \text{FN}_w = |G|$ when $G$ is binary truth and distance-buffered coverage $s_P(x) \in [0, 1]$ acts as effective recall on $G$ and effective false alarm on $S \setminus G$):

$$\text{DTI} = \frac{\text{TP}_w}{\text{TP}_w + \alpha\,\text{FP}_w + \beta\,\text{FN}_w}, \qquad \alpha = 0.2,\; \beta = 0.8.$$

For any perturbation $(\Delta\text{TP}_w, \Delta\text{FP}_w)$ with $\Delta\text{FN}_w = -\Delta\text{TP}_w$, first-order perturbation yields:

$$\Delta\text{DTI} > 0 \iff \Delta\text{TP}_w \cdot \bigl[\alpha\,\text{FP}_w + \beta\,|G|\bigr] > \alpha\,\text{TP}_w\,\Delta\text{FP}_w \iff \frac{\Delta\text{TP}_w}{\Delta\text{TP}_w + \Delta\text{FP}_w} > \tau(\text{DTI}) \equiv \frac{\alpha\,\text{DTI}}{1 + (\beta - \alpha)\,\text{DTI}}.$$

Evaluating $\tau(\text{DTI})$ at the key operating regimes:
- At $\text{DTI} = 0.2600$ (`d2.8` parent): $\tau(0.2600) = \frac{0.052}{1.156} \approx \mathbf{0.04498}$ ($4.50\%$ marginal kernel-weighted precision).
- At $\text{DTI} = 0.2778$ (`B2`): $\tau(0.2778) = \frac{0.05556}{1.16668} \approx \mathbf{0.04762}$ ($4.76\%$ marginal kernel-weighted precision).
- At $\text{DTI} = 0.4413\text{–}0.4544$ (public leaderboard leaders): $\tau(0.45) = \frac{0.090}{1.270} \approx \mathbf{0.07087}$ ($7.09\%$ marginal kernel-weighted precision).

**Why `B2` ($r = 2$) improved upon `d2.8` ($r = 0$) if user reports reflect real scoring:**
1. **Digitization-halo suppression in $0 < d \le 200\text{ m}$:** A detector trained or tuned on geophysical gradients (`tmi_hg`, `det_elev_slope`, `iso_grav_anom_hg`) fires most strongly along *already-mapped* range-front scarps $C$. Although pixels *on* $C$ are masked, pixels at $d \in (0, 200]\text{ m}$ perpendicular to $C$ remain unmasked in $S$ and almost always represent cross-strike width of the *same* already-mapped fault rather than a parallel, unmapped second fault $100\text{–}200\text{ m}$ away. Thus dots in $0 < d \le 200\text{ m}$ incur large $\Delta\text{FP}_w$ on unmasked wall-rock pixels while contributing negligible $\Delta\text{TP}_w$ (their marginal precision falls well below $\tau \approx 4.6\%$).
2. **Kernel overhang preserves junction recall:** Even where a genuine unmapped splay or branch fault $G$ intersects a mapped fault $C$ orthogonally, retaining dots at $d > 200\text{ m}$ with a $300\text{ m}$ triangular kernel still supplies positive kernel weight $K(d)$ into the $100\text{–}200\text{ m}$ junction zone, limiting $\Delta\text{FN}_w$ loss.
3. **Why blind collar expansion ($r \ge 3$) or uncalibrated dot additions (`GEMSDOE36`) hit diminishing/negative returns:** Beyond $200\text{–}300\text{ m}$, triangular overhang from outer dots vanishes ($K(300\text{ m}) = 0$), severing real fault-tip extensions and en-echelon relays; conversely, `GEMSDOE36`'s `+1,200` PINN dots reduced user-reported DTI (`0.2778 -> 0.2750`) because their marginal kernel-weighted precision fell below $\tau(0.2778) = 4.76\%$.
