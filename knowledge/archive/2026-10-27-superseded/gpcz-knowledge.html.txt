<h2>3. Geology used by the GPC-Z lane</h2>
<table>
<tr><th>Concept</th><th>Statement</th><th>Provenance</th></tr>
<tr><td>Basin-and-Range normal faults</td><td>Range-front normal faults dip basin-ward; the hanging wall (basin) is down-dropped, so the scarp&#x27;s free face <b>faces the basin</b>. Fault damage zones and synthetic/antithetic splays occur within a few hundred metres to a few kilometres of the master trace.</td><td>TEXTBOOK (e.g., Stewart 1980 &quot;Geology of Nevada&quot;; Faulds &amp; Varga 1998 on accommodation zones)</td></tr>
<tr><td>Gravity polarity</td><td>Basin fill is less dense than bedrock; the isostatic residual gravity gradient and the depth-to-basement surface point toward the basin depocentre. A topographic step whose downhill direction agrees with the gravity/basement deepening direction is <b>polarity-concordant</b> with a normal fault; a step facing the other way is more likely a terrace riser, shoreline berm, road cut or levee.</td><td>TEXTBOOK (Blakely 1995 &quot;Potential Theory in Gravity and Magnetic Applications&quot;; Jachens &amp; Moring 1990 basin-depth maps of Nevada)</td></tr>
<tr><td>Why faults are off-catalogue</td><td>INGENIOUS compiled existing maps (mostly 1:250k–1:100k); short (&lt; 2–3 km), low (&lt; 5 m) and partly buried strands next to mapped master faults are the typical omissions found when 1 m LiDAR becomes available. Power-law fault-length scaling implies a deficit of short faults in regional compilations.</td><td>TEXTBOOK (Scholz 2019 &quot;The Mechanics of Earthquakes and Faulting&quot;, fault-population scaling); SIBLING (19GEMSDOE power-law budget note)</td></tr>
<tr><td>Geothermal relevance</td><td>Great Basin geothermal systems concentrate at fault terminations, step-overs, accommodation zones and intersections where permeability is maintained; mapping missed strands near known ones is precisely the expert task described in the rules.</td><td>TEXTBOOK (Faulds et al. 2011, GRC Transactions; Faulds &amp; Hinz 2015 play-fairway)</td></tr>
<tr><td>Named mimics (non-fault processes producing the same signal)</td><td>Lake Lahontan and other pluvial shoreline berms (contour-parallel steps, often basin-facing!), alluvial-fan terrace risers, piedmont roads/canals/railroad grades, playa margins. The polarity gate removes the away-facing half; the near-catalogue prior reduces exposure to isolated shorelines; <b>basin-facing shorelines near faults remain an unmitigated risk</b> (IR-K1-OBLIQ-01).</td><td>TEXTBOOK (Reheis 1999 pluvial lake maps); MEASURED (H2 falsified)</td></tr>
</table>
