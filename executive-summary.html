H33 forensic interpretation: plausible mechanism, unproven cause

The strongest source-supported explanation is an emission/spacing effect in a nested H19-5 family—not a demonstrated new geological discovery mechanism.

The source repository's H33 forensic note treats the reported result as OWNER-REPORT and says no submission-page receipt binds it to the raster bytes. Its reconstruction places H33-2-B2 at the end of a thinning/pruning ladder: a dense lineament field was spatially thinned, then predictions near already mapped faults were removed by a buffer rule. The report's proposed explanation is that closely spaced dots reuse credit under the requested 300 m triangular-kernel protocol while still adding prediction mass; pruning can reduce redundant or low-credit dots, improving precision per emitted dot. This is a plausible effect of the metric and the artifact geometry, not evidence that the retained dots are true uncatalogued faults.

The available H33 forensic note explicitly says the historical value is not a HOLDOUT-DTI result and does not provide a versioned evaluator, withheld-positive count, or confidence interval for that result. That means the public evidence does not establish controlled hidden-segment recovery for H33. This is an evidence gap, not a claim that the original holdout implementation leaked.

Several earlier inversion, hidden-truth, recall, and score-ceiling estimates have been withdrawn by the source analysis. No causal attribution, current leaderboard comparison, or new live-score estimate is justified here. The correct next step is reproducible, fold-local holdout evaluation—not copying H33 or rebranding a nested family as novel.

Hypothesis screen: research gaps, not certified novelty

The first draft's generic edge, basement-step, strain-transition, and terrain-gap hypotheses were removed from the “untried” list: the source project records overlapping H33/H36/H48 work, and the nine-site histories include related methods. The four concepts below are documented as unexecuted or data-blocked in the inspected GEMSDOE32 H48 pre-registration. They are not certified novel across every sibling repository; no implementation or lane selection is authorized. Upside and cost are qualitative rankings, not score projections.

1 · Highest scientific upsideVery high / data blocked

Deep magnetotelluric root beneath a shallow clay cap

Layers: competition band 17 (cond_surf) and band 15 (depth_to_base_surf), plus multi-depth 3-D MT resistivity slices from the GBCGE/DOE INGENIOUS data effort or an official USGS ScienceBase MT release.

Physical signature: a shallow conductive smectite/clay cap over a steep, deeper resistivity boundary/root, with playa-like closed basins masked. This targets fluid pathways whose surface trace is buried or absent.

Named non-fault mimic: saline playa brines or a conductive stratigraphic horizon. Difference: the inspected GEMSDOE32 notes describe only a shallow single-slice conductivity treatment; the proposed test couples shallow alteration to a deep 3-D structure.

Availability: the cited free official source family is identified in the source preregistration, but the actual volume was not retrieved or verified and the project report says those endpoints are outside this sandbox's egress allowlist. Not viable in this run.

2 · High scientific upsideHigh / data blocked

Relocated seismic planes projected up-dip, corroborated by InSAR

Layers/data: the free USGS ANSS ComCat FDSN event service or a relocated regional catalog (including the Monte Cristo study), plus NASA Alaska Satellite Facility Sentinel-1 products and ARIA products.

Physical signature: fit spatially coherent 3-D hypocenter planes, project the planes up-dip, and require independent linear displacement/phase-gradient support. Active blind strands may be missing from a static mapped-fault compilation.

Named non-fault mimic: quarry blasts, non-planar earthquake swarms, or non-tectonic subsidence. Difference: this is a relocated-plane plus surface-deformation test, not a broad smoothed seismicity band or an epicentral heat map.

Availability: the specific free sources named are the USGS FDSN endpoint, the cited Monte Cristo catalog (DOI 10.1785/0220200371), and NASA ASF/ARIA. The inspected project report says these external endpoints are blocked in this sandbox; files were not independently fetched. Not viable in this run.

3 · Best local-data research gapModerate / no new data

Anisotropic minimum-action bridges between scarp terminations

Layers: 1 m LiDAR scarp strength/strike/coherence, detrended elevation and slope, and gravity-gradient support already described in the project records.

Physical signature: solve an anisotropic Eikonal/geodesic path between exposed scarp tips, assigning low traversal cost to aligned, coherent scarp and subsurface-gradient evidence and high cost across unsupported fan or shoreline surfaces. It targets mapped traces fragmented by alluvial burial.

Named non-fault mimic: Lake Lahontan shoreline benches, ephemeral channels, and roads. Difference: the inspected pre-registration distinguishes continuous minimum-action paths from local radial tip cones; related gap-closing/topology work elsewhere means cross-site novelty remains uncertain.

Availability and rank: no new external dataset is required, but the shared feature stack, evaluator, and raster are absent here. Potential is moderate and test cost moderate; no DTI gain is estimated.

4 · Lower priority / overlap riskLow–moderate / local

Euler contact-depth clusters conditioned on basement depth

Layers: RTP magnetic field, magnetic/gravity vertical derivatives, gravity anomaly/gradient, and depth-to-basement (the cited feature bands 2, 9, 11, 13, 15, and 18).

Physical signature: solve Euler homogeneity windows for contact-like source locations, retain compact solutions whose inferred depth agrees with the basement model, then test whether the clusters trace blind basement/alluvium faults.

Named non-fault mimic: pluton margins, dikes, or volcanic contacts. Difference and prior art: the depth-conditioned filter was deferred in the inspected H48 pre-registration, but Euler-depth methods already appear in other portfolio records (including the cited GEMSDOE40 history). It is therefore not certified untried and is lower priority.

Availability: uses existing competition layers; no external data is needed. This checkout still lacks the inputs and shared tools required to test it.

Selection decision: no single method lane is selected. The available request refers to a permitted “single method paragraph,” but that paragraph is not identified in the current project instructions. The two higher-upside concepts are data-blocked; the local-data concepts have cross-site prior-art risk. Do not implement or spend an experiment until the lane is resolved and the shared template inputs/tools are restored.

Non-negotiable validation and release gates

  • Holdout: withhold complete fault segments plus a buffer; derive catalogue features only from fold-visible faults; pixel-exactly mask visible faults.
  • Leakage: evaluate every feature alone; a two-sided AUC above 0.90 is leakage until proven otherwise.
  • Score evidence: actual results must be labeled HOLDOUT-DTI with evaluator version, withheld-positive count, and 95% CI, or ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED copied from a submission receipt. Never report a score projection.
  • Uniqueness: compare full surfaces and final dots against every registry raster; stop and log a duplicate if rank-correlation exceeds 0.90 or over 70% of dots lie within 3 pixels of a registry raster.
  • Raster validation: use the shared submission writer and real sample GeoTIFF; require no NaN/Inf in the footprint, values in [0,1], and matching CRS, shape, and transform. Inspect irregularities automatically, not by human judgment.
  • Budget and selector: at most three experiments or two hours; a separate selector controls any weekly submission slot. No slot is used by this project.
