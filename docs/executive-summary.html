2. Why thinning could improve DTI (without proving it did)

The official DrivenData metric is a distance-weighted Tversky index with α=0.2 (FP cost), β=0.8 (FN cost), and a 300 m triangular kernel:

DTI = T / (T + α·F + β·(G − T) + ε)

where T = weighted true-positive coverage, F = weighted false-positive mass, G = number of hidden positive pixels.

Consider a deletion that loses L ≥ 0 units of true-positive coverage and removes c ≥ 0 units of weighted false-positive mass. The deletion improves the ratio only when:

α·T·c > L · [α·F + β·G + ε]

This explains why a 2-px collar thinning can help: it removes low-yield predictions near mapped faults (where known-fault pixels are masked in scoring per the DrivenData staff clarification) while reducing false-positive cost. The reported sequence (parent → 1-px collar → 2-px collar → +1,200 dots) is consistent with this mechanism but is not a controlled causal experiment — no evaluator receipts or fold assignments are available.

3. Can a new result beat 0.2778?

Possible in principle; not established or forecast here. The reported 0.2778 is a single USER-REPORTED value with no receipt. To beat it credibly, a new submission would need:

  • Holdout evidence: HOLDOUT-DTI with 95% CI on a spatially-blocked hide-and-recover holdout, using the same evaluator as the reference solution.
  • Uniqueness: ≤70% forward 3-px overlap vs every registry raster, |Spearman| ≤0.90.
  • Organizer confirmation: a submission-page receipt from DrivenData.

Structural barrier under the current protocol: the 13GEMSDOE lattice covers 99.87% of the footprint within 3 px. With a ~37,654-dot budget (B2's scale), at least ~82.3% of dots must fall within 3 px of the lattice — necessarily exceeding the 70% duplicate threshold. Any dot-based submission at that scale is a lattice duplicate by construction. To be both unique and high-scoring requires either (a) far fewer dots placed in novel territory (which sacrifices DTI coverage) or (b) a fundamentally different, non-dot prediction surface.

Bottom line: we cannot generate a verified score higher than 0.2778 without organizer data. The unique TIF generated here (1,322 dots in novel territory) is format-valid and lattice-unique but is not expected to be competitive on the leaderboard — its small dot count, necessary for uniqueness, cannot achieve the TP coverage needed for a high DTI. It is published for research and audit only.
