## Why GEMSDOE32's reported 0.2778 might outperform its family

**USER-REPORTED, not ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED:** 0.2778 is the largest of the nine historical figures supplied by the user, not a new measured score or a currently verified leaderboard maximum. The upstream account describes a shared H19-5 family, thinning to separated dots and pruning near known faults (B2). With a distance-tolerant kernel, redundant predictions can add false-positive mass after nearby truth is already covered; removing redundancy can improve the TP/FP/FN balance. Masking known faults must match the official evaluator exactly. These mechanisms explain a plausible family trend, not why private hidden truth awarded a particular number.

The locally repeated byte audit proves geometry/range facts and extensive spatial overlap. It does **not** reveal private truth, establish causality, verify receipts, or calibrate a future public-score estimate. A different TIFF SHA can reflect packaging or NaN encoding rather than a different prediction map. Final-dot similarity is therefore required in addition to hashes.

A higher public score is possible in principle, but neither 0.2778 nor the prompt's conflicting 0.3195/0.4413 leaderboard claims supports a numerical prediction for a new strategy. No organizer-confirmed result was accessible here. Fault-trace prediction is the submission target; geothermal vents are a motivation, not equivalent supervised labels.

## Literal lattice irregularity — independently measured

`evidence/historical_byte_audit.json` re-reads the nine pinned best TIFFs, checks SHA-256 against upstream, compares template geometry and value ranges, and calculates directed spatial overlaps. The additional pinned 13GEMSDOE lattice has SHA-256 `b4570b406efec198eb2f4d142ad8d0d6184b14b66f2f52b5c4d697f0647dc9b2`.

Its 3-pixel neighborhood covers 99.871327% of the 5,167,373-pixel finite template footprint, leaving 6,649 cells outside. To have no more than 70% of N distinct dots covered, at least 0.30N must be outside; necessarily N <= 6,649 / 0.30, hence at most 22,163 dots. This is a necessary, not sufficient, condition and says nothing about geological value or score. All nine historical best files have over 99.9% overlap with that lattice and fail the literal rule. This does not mean they are exact pixel duplicates. Dense-registry overlap can make the rule nonspecific; only an explicit protocol revision could change it. It must not be silently waived.
