SCIENCE BEFORE SCORING

Understand the ground.
Respect the unknown.

The competition predicts faults that may indicate geothermal resources. A predicted fault is not, by itself, a discovered geothermal vent.

ⓘ

Research status: The four ideas below are untested proposals. Their ranking is qualitative, not a projected score. No assigned method lane or approved TIFF exists. These proposals were not implemented in this review; historical peers may have explored related mechanisms.

WHAT WE CAN INFER

Why did the B2 family look promising?

The supplied GEMSDOE32 result of 0.2778 USER-REPORTED / UNVERIFIED has no organizer receipt here. A better result is possible, but has not been demonstrated.

Our byte-pinned raster audit shows the B2 mask is exactly the GEMSDOE25 d2.8 parent after deleting 6,436 dots within two pixels of the mirrored fault catalogue. The two files are not independent detectors.

Reducing redundant false-positive mass can improve distance-weighted Tversky, but removing points can also lose true-positive coverage. Without the hidden labels we cannot identify the cause of the reported score.

Important caveat: DrivenData staff say the known-fault mask is pixel-exact; new faults can still occur nearby. Near-catalogue predictions are not automatically false positives. Read the staff clarification ↗

PROPOSED, NOT EXECUTED

Four hypotheses to investigate.

Ordered by qualitative promise and cost. All require a lane assignment, input verification and leakage-safe holdout. Novelty across peer sites is not established.

Full research notes ↗
01 MEDIUM COST

Cross-field edge displacement

Measure signed cross-profile displacement between magnetic and gravity edges, persistently along strike, to test for buried offsets rather than only co-located ridge strength.

Could mimic: volcanic contacts or survey-leveling artifacts.
Differs from HMAS gradient persistence and GPC-Z concordance; exact fleet-wide novelty unknown. Cached bands absent.
02 MEDIUM COST

Alteration-boundary alignment

Trace local K/eTh boundary segments and test their termination or displacement at a magnetic edge; HMAS instead uses K/Th halo intensity.

Could mimic: potassium-rich intrusions, soil and weathering.
USGS advertises GeoDAWN grids; official data bytes not fetched.
03 HIGH COST

Repeated drainage asymmetry

Test directional consistency of tributary cross-valley asymmetry from USGS 3DEP, conditioned on drainage area and elevation.

Could mimic: stream piracy, erosion and alluvial fans.
USGS data advertised free; local tiles not verified.
04 HIGH COST

Sampling-aware thermal residuals

Cross-fit shallow probe residuals against access and terrain, then test nearby field boundaries; HMAS used raw plume features.

Could mimic: solar heating, groundwater or cultural heat.
GDR probe archive listed; coverage not verified here.
