4 · GEMSDOE32 AT 0.2778

Why this file scored 0.2778, and whether 0.2778 can be beaten.

Owner-reported. No organiser receipt ties 0.2778 to this hash. The mechanism below is derived from the official metric and measured on the pinned rasters.

What the metric rewards

The official DTI (DrivenData problem page) is TPw / (TPw + αFPw + βFNw + ε), with α=0.2, β=0.8, and a triangular kernel k(d)=max(1−d/300 m, 0). The worked example on the page (TP 3.00, FP 1.89, FN 2.00) gives ≈0.60, and vendor/gems57/metric.py matches it.

What GEMSDOE32 changed relative to GEMSDOE25/30 (measured)

The GEMSDOE30 parent has 44,090 dots; GEMSDOE25 has the same positive set. GEMSDOE32 has 37,654 dots, all of which belong to the parent (subset check: true). It removes 6,436 parent dots within 2 px of the catalogue (3,891 of them at 1 px) and adds none. Subset and removal counts are measured on the pinned rasters (subset_checks in the focus-site table JSON); the reason for the removal is inferred.

The marginal rule (exact in the binary-dot model)

Let a new binary dot have marginal credit c = max(0, k − Mg) on its best truth g (where Mg is the current best kernel hit on g), and miss share m = 1 − maxg k. Because α + β = 1, the denominator changes by exactly α(m + c), and the numerator by c. The dot improves DTI if and only if c / (m + c) > α · DTI. At an owner-reported DTI scale of 0.26, the threshold is ≈0.052. For a fresh withheld pixel at distance d, this means a dot is worth emitting only when d ≲ 3 × (1 − 0.052) ≈ 2.84 px. Dots farther away and dots next to already-hit truth have c≈0 and lower the score. Dots next to visible catalogue earn no credit only if hidden faults are rarely adjacent to visible ones; that assumption is not measured here.

What this implies for 0.2778

Removing near-catalogue dots lowers FP mass and costs little credit, so the score can rise. This is consistent with the owner's +0.0178 step from 0.2600 to 0.2778, but that step is unverified. The rule also says the gain comes from removing zero-credit dots, not from adding more.

Why our own holdout disagrees

Our holdout withholds catalogue segments, so pixels next to visible faults earn credit by construction. It therefore rewards proximity, the opposite of the collar. This is a structural limit of the holdout, not evidence about hidden faults. The holdout and the owner's leaderboard step point in different directions, and the evidence here cannot settle which one reflects the organiser's hidden set.

Can a higher score be achieved?

The organiser's leaderboard shows far higher scores exist (0.4544 at the top, live check). The rule says to add dots only where they have credit above α·DTI, meaning distinct coverage of unhit truth rather than clustered high-probability pixels. This session produced no candidate that shows it. H4 below is the untested test. Not established: whether 0.2778 can be beaten on the organiser's set.
