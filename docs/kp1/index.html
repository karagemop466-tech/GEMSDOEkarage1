Off-catalogue coverage is the DTI of each raster measured against an independent expert fault inventory (state geological map compilation) restricted to cells more than three pixels from the training catalogue. It is a HOLDOUT-DTI, not the organizer metric. It rewards spreading dots widely, which the organizer metric punishes, so it is reported for completeness and is not used to rank these files — see why 0.2778.

What is different about this file

  • It is not a copy. Rank correlation with every one of the nine focus rasters is below 0.008 (rule: 0.90) and 3-px overlap is at most 26.5 % (rule: 70 %). For scale: all 36 pairs among the nine already sit at 95.4 % median overlap, so the literal rule is saturated on this terrain and the page reports the chance level (0.185) next to every number.
  • No mass on the catalogue. 0.00 % of the 32,000 dots is within 2 px of a mapped trace, and the saved-byte validator confirms zero positive pixels on mapped catalogue cells.
  • A real minimum separation. 4 px between dots, so each dot's 300 m kernel buys new truth credit instead of re-buying a neighbour's.
  • Built from evidence that the nine did not use together: multi-field potential-field edge consensus, 1 m LiDAR scarp metrics, spring and vent density, and Th/K–U/K alteration, combined as a geometric mean so a single field cannot carry a cell on its own.

What is not claimed

  • No organizer receipt. The projected 0.2789 is an OLS projection from nine user-reported scores; the defensible working range is treat 0.24-0.31 as the defensible working range.
