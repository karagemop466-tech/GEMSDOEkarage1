Research: five candidate mechanisms, ranked

Required by the standing brief: name the layers, the physical signature, why it should catch a fault that is missing from the catalogue rather than one already in it, how it differs from what this repository has already run, and the non-fault process that could mimic it. Ranking is by (expected DTI gain) ÷ (implementation cost); only the first was validated this session.

1. K-PROXY-DETECT / TTCA — thermo-tectonic conduit alignment (built this session)

  • Layers: 19-band stack (TMI horizontal gradient, tilt derivative, isostatic-gravity horizontal gradient, detrended-elevation slope and curvature, strain second invariant, conductivity, depth-to-basement) plus four layers that are not in the organizer stack: the TMI upward-continued to 150 m (channel 4 of the pinned GeoDAWN extensions product), the contractor Th/K, U/K and U/Th ratio grids (channels 1–3), the 12-band 1 m LiDAR scarp product, and a kernel density of 27,092 thermal-spring and well records.
  • Signature: geometric-mean consensus of (i) multi-field edge agreement — a boundary seen by magnetics, gravity, the deep magnetic field and the basement step at the same place, (ii) scarp/curvature evidence, (iii) fluid-conduit density, (iv) K-metasomatism (low Th/K, low U/K), (v) strain.
  • Why it targets unmapped faults: upward continuation suppresses the near-surface magnetic texture that the mapped traces sit on and leaves deeper basement structure visible; Th/K and U/K map hydrothermal alteration halos, which are not in the catalogue at all; springs discharge on fault damage zones. None of these four layers is present in the 19-band stack, and the emission is forbidden inside the 2-px catalogue corridor.
  • Differs from this repository's history: previous lanes either fitted a distance-to-catalogue halo (anatomy/relay/stepover families) or ran a supervised classifier on catalogue labels and then emitted near them. This lane never uses a catalogue-derived predictor for the surface, never emits a fixed lattice, and its support overlap with the whole registry family is 27% instead of the family's 97.5–100 %.
  • Non-fault mimics: playa clay mineralogy (low Th/K without alteration), Quaternary basalt (high K, strong magnetic response), upward-continuation rings around large magnetic bodies, springs at permeable basin-fill contacts, and road cuts in the LiDAR scarp metrics.
  • Cost: low — four new pinned layers, no training run, ~3 minutes to build.

2. Spring-lineament intersection census (not built)

Layers: spring/well records × mapped and inferred trace azimuths. Signature: excess spring density at trace intersections and at fault tips, compared with along-strike mid-segments. Why off-catalogue: an intersection of two mapped faults is itself frequently unmapped. Differs: uses the topology of the trace network rather than a distance halo. Mimic: permeable contacts at range fronts. Cost: medium — needs a trace-topology build. Ranked 2 because it is orthogonal to the surface built here and could be fused, but could not be validated this session.

3. Upward-continuation depth series (150 m / 300 m / 500 m) for concealed faults (not built)

Only the 150 m product is pinned locally; a depth series would separate shallow volcanic texture from deep basement fabric and target structures under basin fill. Why off-catalogue: basin-covered structures have no surface trace. Mimic: continuation ringing. Cost: medium — the 300/500 m levels would have to be derived from the official grids (USGS sciencebase item 657e1d85d34e23d3533209f7, files 22103_area1_tiffs.zip / 22103_area2_tiffs.zip, sizes recorded in data/external/observed_files.json), which are not reachable from this sandbox's network allowlist — so this idea is named as viable but currently unobtainable.

4. Fault-tip damage-zone extrapolation from Qfaults kinematics (partly built, not promoted)

Layers: gdr_qfaults_traces.csv (slip rate, recency, dip direction, sense) × magnetic and gravity gradients. Signature: along-strike continuation beyond a mapped tip, where slip rate and recency imply the rupture continues. Why off-catalogue: catalogue traces stop where mapping stopped, not where the fault stops. Mimic: depositional pinch-outs. Cost: high — and it is close to lanes already executed in sibling repositories (relay/stepover/tip-extrapolation), which is why it is ranked last despite a strong mechanism.

5. Earthquake-relocation clustering across mapped gaps (not built)

Layers: ieq_n100a15, deq_n100a15 and the strain-rate bands. Signature: microseismicity aligned across a gap between mapped traces. Why off-catalogue: seismicity is independent of the mapping. Mimic: mining blasts and geothermal-field pumping. Cost: high — the provided seismicity bands are pre-smoothed density fields, so the alignment test would need the raw catalogue, which is not pinned here.

Instrument calibration — a recorded negative result

The shared hide-and-recover evaluator (gems57-pooled-hide-v2) gives the nine focus files DTIs that are perfectly anti-correlated with their known leaderboard scores (Spearman -1.0). Recommendation for the protocol owner: stop using catalogue-recovery DTI as a promotion gate for this competition, or replace it with an instrument calibrated on known score pairs. Until then, promotion should rest on the geometry table (Table 1 and the covariate analysis) plus saved-byte gates, and every number should be labelled as a projection.
