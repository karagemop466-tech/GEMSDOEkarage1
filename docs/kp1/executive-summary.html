“Estimated score” has exactly one honest meaning here: a projection from a model fitted to the nine user-reported scores, not a measurement. The organizer's scored truth is a private set of expert-identified new faults; it is not in this repository, so no file can be *scored* before upload. See the projection section for the model, its leave-one-out error and its caveats.

Why GEMSDOE32 B2 (0.2778) was the highest of the nine

The published metric reduces exactly, because β = 1 − α = 0.8:

DTI = TPw / ( α(TPw + FPw) + β|G| )

with TPw the kernel-weighted credit your dots earn on the organizer's private truth, FPw the mass that earns nothing, and |G| the (fixed, unknown) number of truth pixels. Three consequences follow, and the nine known scores obey all three:

  1. Mass near the mapped catalogue is wasted. The scored truth is a set of faults that is not in the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue (competition page 967), so a dot 0–2 px from a mapped trace earns nothing but still costs 0.2 in the denominator. B2 and GEMSDOE36 (0.2778, 0.2750) place 0.0 % of their dots within 2 px of the catalogue; the three lowest-scoring files place 14.5 %.
  2. Redundant dots are pure cost. Two dots inside each other's 3-px kernel can spend the same truth cell's credit twice while each adds one unit of FP mass. B2 has the lowest 3-px clumping of the nine (0.26 %); GEMSDOE36's 42.6 % clumping is the documented reason its otherwise good file fell to 0.1647 in the fleet's own autopsy.
  3. Fewer, better-placed dots beat more dots. Across the nine, score is monotone decreasing in dot count (ρ = −0.98) and monotone increasing in the fraction of dots more than 15 px (1.5 km) from the catalogue (ρ = +1.00). B2 is the smallest of the nine (37,654 dots) and the furthest out (58.4 % > 15 px).

Caveat stated plainly: n = 9 and the covariates are strongly collinear, so these are associations that are consistent with the metric algebra, not isolated causal effects. They are the only quantitative bridge between a raster's geometry and a leaderboard number that this project has.

The projection, and what it does and does not mean

Model: OLS of the nine user-reported scores on [log(dots), fraction of dots > 15 px from the catalogue]. Leave-one-out RMSE 0.0014. Applied to this candidate it returns 0.2789 (bootstrap 95 % [0.276, 0.285]), against 0.2769 for the same model applied to B2 — a Δ of +0.0020. The candidate sits at 32,000 dots (outside the observed range 37,654–61,328, i.e. an extrapolation) and 55.5% of dots > 15 px (inside the observed range 58.4%–49.6%).

Metric inversion: how good does a submission have to be?

Solving the metric for the fraction h of dots that must land within ~1 px of an expert new-fault pixel to reproduce 0.2778 with B2's 37,654 dots gives 5.9% if the private truth is 10,000 px, 21.5% at 60,988 px, and 39.5% at 120,000 px. |G| is unpublished, so this is a bracket, not a measurement — but it sizes the bar: the champion's surface must already be converting roughly one dot in five to ten into a hit, and a new candidate has to do at least as well with fewer dots.
