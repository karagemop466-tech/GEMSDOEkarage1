Executive summary

Nine submissions, one explanation, one new file

Bottom line

The nine focus submissions are not nine ideas. They are nine points on a one-parameter family that covers the same 2 %-of-the-map lineament support with different numbers of dots. Their scores rise as their dot count falls, and the best of them — GEMSDOE32's h33-h33-2-b2-...-zeros at 0.2778 — is the smallest member. This session builds a file that is not a member of that family, verifies it against the official format rules and the uniqueness rule, and publishes the one number that matters next to the one thing that is missing: an organizer receipt.

Why the best of the nine is 0.2778

Across the nine, reported score tracks dot count at Spearman -0.967 (slope -0.0012 per 1,000 dots) and nothing else does. The metric charges every dot that is not exactly on a truth cell alpha = 0.2, and pays out only what lands within 300 m. The nine files sit at the point where one more dot is just below break-even, so deleting dots helps — which is exactly what the B2 lineage did, and the reason its score is the highest of the nine and only 0.0011 above the projection for a file with 5,654 fewer dots. Full arithmetic: Why 0.2778.

  • Unique: max |Spearman| 0.0076, max 3-px overlap 26.5 % against the nine (limits 0.90 and 70 %).
  • Better than random on an independent inventory: on the state-compilation faults that are absent from the training catalogue, the candidate covers +19.3 % more fault mass than a random dot field of the same size (block bootstrap: +0.0109, 95 % [-0.0034, +0.0260], 92 % of draws positive). Its 32,000 dots buy what 40,171 random dots would.
  • Projected: 0.2789 (LOO interval [0.2762, 0.2816]; parameter bootstrap [0.2757, 0.2846]), i.e. +0.0020 versus B2. PROJECTED, not a score.

What would change the answer

  1. An organizer receipt for the upload. It replaces the projection with a measurement and is the only thing that can start a real calibration curve.
  2. A surface whose per-dot truth rate clears 0.0588 (the corrected marginal bar at 0.2778; an earlier 0.0769 was an algebra error). Nothing in this repository has demonstrated that; two different local instruments disagree with the leaderboard, which is itself the most important result of the session.
