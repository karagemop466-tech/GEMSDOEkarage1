Metric analysis

Why 0.2778, and what it would take to beat it

The published metric, for a binary dot field, reduces exactly to

DTI = T / ( alpha·(n − M) + beta·|G| + T ), alpha = 0.2, beta = 0.8

with n dots, T the truth credit they cover, M their own self-credit and |G| the number of truth cells. Two consequences decide everything:

  • a dot that lands on a truth cell is free (its own credit cancels its false-positive mass) and adds up to 1.0 to T;
  • a dot that misses costs exactly 0.2 whatever else is true.

So one more dot helps iff its expected truth credit clears

dT > alpha·(1 − m)·s / (1 − s)

which depends only on the current score s. At 0.2778 the bar is 0.0769; at 0.3195 it is 0.0939; at 0.4413, 0.1580. A field that is already at 0.2778 must find new dots that are right about 7.7 % of the time; to reach the leaderboard top it would need 15.8 %.

What the nine files vary

Reported score against dot count across the nine: Spearman -0.967, slope -0.0012 per 1,000 dots. Nothing else in the measured covariate table moves the score monotonically except the fraction of dots that are far from the catalogue (which is collinear with dot count within this family). That is the evidence for "nine points on one family", and it is why 0.2778 is a position rather than an idea.

The ceiling, and what the leaderboard numbers imply

A dot field with perfect knowledge cannot exceed 1/(1+beta) = 0.556 once every truth cell is covered: the false-negative weight is a hard tax on an un-covered truth cell. That places 0.4544 at 82 % of the theoretical maximum of the submission format.

What this candidate's surface has to achieve

At fixed surface quality more dots is always better, so no submission wins on dot count alone. Two anomalies the session could not resolve

  1. Two independent local instruments (catalogue leave-out, and the state-compilation inventory) both disagree with the observed leaderboard ordering. That means the private truth is not recoverable from the public catalogue by any method tested here, and any local "holdout DTI" is a statement about the instrument, not about the submission.
  2. The literal 3-px / 70 % uniqueness rule is saturated: all 36 pairs among the nine focus rasters score 86.7–100.0 % against a chance level near 15 %. Under a literal reading of that rule the nine are all duplicates of each other, which cannot be what the rule intends.
