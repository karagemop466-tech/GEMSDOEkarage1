WHAT THE MIRRORED BYTES SHOW

Why B2 may have differed.

Binary-mask algebra on the hash-pinned mirrors shows GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 share a 44,090-dot parent mask. GEMSDOE32 B2 is a subset after removing 6,436 dots within two pixels of the mirrored known-fault catalogue; it adds no new dots. GEMSDOE31 and GEMSDOE28 h27-4-r1 are a milder one-pixel collar-ablation variant that removes 3,891 dots. GEMSDOE36 adds 1,200 off-catalogue dots to the B2-sized mask.

The associated values—0.2708, 0.2778 for GEMSDOE32 B2, and 0.2750—are USER-REPORTED / UNVERIFIED context only: no artifact-linked organizer receipt or reproducible HOLDOUT-DTI report with evaluator, withheld-positive count, and 95% CI was verified in this review. They are not presented as validated scores.

A collar ablation could alter a distance-weighted Tversky tradeoff if the saved false-positive penalty outweighs recall lost near known faults. It could also remove real target coverage. Mask algebra explains the difference in the masks, not why a reported score changed or whether the reported ranking is real. A catalogue buffer is not automatically a free scoring exclusion. 01 TOP PROPOSAL · MEDIUM COST

Cross-spectral potential-field junctions

Test multi-wavelength phase coherence and shared edge normals between GeoDAWN magnetics, isostatic gravity, and basement-depth gradients. Buried fault steps may persist under cover without a surface trace.

Named mimic: intrusive/lithologic contacts, volcanic boundaries, or survey seams.
Potential: highest qualitative in this slate; no measured uplift. Adds cross-field phase congruence and junction topology beyond existing amplitude/gradient ridges. Requires the missing organizer stack and assigned lane.
02 HIGH COST

Joint density–susceptibility interface inversion

Fit a low-complexity shared faulted-basement interface to gravity and magnetic observations with separate physical contrasts and explicit uncertainty.

Named mimic: volcanic body, lithologic contact, or multiple-source superposition.
Potential: medium qualitative, high non-uniqueness risk. Distinct from current derivative/edge features; requires synthetic recovery and shared holdout.
03 HIGH DATA COST · NOT VIABLE YET

Stratigraphic-contact offset consistency

Reward displacement or truncation of a mapped unit contact—not the contact itself—where it independently aligns with geophysical structure.

Named mimic: erosional unconformity, depositional pinch-out, or cartographic boundary.
Potential: low-to-medium qualitative; high data cost. USGS National Geologic Map Database is the official source to check, but coverage, vector downloads, license, and grid registration were not verified here; not data-ready.
04 HIGH COST · NOT VIABLE YET

Inter-catchment knickpoint recurrence

Test whether area-normalized channel-breaks recur at a common basement position across adjacent catchments, rather than scoring a single scarp.

Named mimic: resistant-bed contact, stream capture, fan incision, or pluvial shoreline bench.
Potential: low-to-medium qualitative; high cost. USGS 3DEP tile coverage and payload access were not verified; the earlier drainage-asymmetry concept makes lane novelty uncertain.
